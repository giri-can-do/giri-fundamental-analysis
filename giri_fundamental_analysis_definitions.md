# Giri Fundamental Analysis Tool --- Definitions & Formula Reference

## Purpose

This document defines the financial metrics, formulas, and
interpretation principles used in the Fundamental Analysis Tool V1.

The tool uses SEC EDGAR Company Facts / XBRL data as its primary source
for company financial statements. Values should be analyzed over
multiple years and in context; no single ratio is treated as an
automatic buy/sell signal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Income Statement Metrics

## Revenue

**Definition:** Total income generated from the company's ordinary
business activities before expenses are deducted.

**Typical SEC XBRL concepts:** -
`RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` - `Revenues` -
`SalesRevenueNet`

**Why it matters:** Shows the scale of the business and whether customer
demand/business activity is growing.

### Revenue Growth / YoY Growth

**Formula:**

`YoY Growth (%) = ((Current Year Revenue - Previous Year Revenue) / Previous Year Revenue) × 100`

**Interpretation:** Positive growth means revenue increased versus the
prior fiscal year. Analyze consistency and acceleration/deceleration
rather than one year in isolation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Gross Profit

**Definition:** Revenue remaining after direct costs associated with
producing or delivering products/services.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `GrossProfit`

### Gross Margin

**Formula:**

`Gross Margin (%) = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100`

**Interpretation:** Shows how much gross profit the company retains from
each dollar of revenue before operating expenses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Operating Income

**Definition:** Profit generated by the core business after operating
expenses but before certain non-operating items and taxes.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `OperatingIncomeLoss`

### Operating Margin

**Formula:**

`Operating Margin (%) = Operating Income / Revenue × 100`

**Interpretation:** Measures the profitability and operating efficiency
of the core business.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Net Income

**Definition:** Profit remaining after operating expenses, interest,
taxes, and other applicable income/expenses.

**Typical SEC XBRL concepts:** - `NetIncomeLoss` - `ProfitLoss`

### Net Margin

**Formula:**

`Net Margin (%) = Net Income / Revenue × 100`

**Interpretation:** Shows how much of each dollar of revenue ultimately
becomes net profit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Diluted EPS

**Definition:** Net earnings attributable per diluted share,
incorporating the potential dilution from instruments such as options or
convertible securities where applicable.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `EarningsPerShareDiluted`

### EPS Growth

**Formula:**

`EPS Growth (%) = ((Current EPS - Previous EPS) / |Previous EPS|) × 100`

**Interpretation:** Shows the change in earnings attributable per
diluted share. EPS can be affected by both changes in earnings and
changes in diluted share count.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Balance Sheet Metrics

Unlike the Income Statement and Cash Flow Statement, which cover a
period of time, Balance Sheet values represent a financial position at a
specific reporting date.

## Cash & Cash Equivalents

**Definition:** Highly liquid cash resources available to the company.

**Typical SEC XBRL concepts:** -
`CashAndCashEquivalentsAtCarryingValue` -
`CashCashEquivalentsRestrictedCashAndRestrictedCashEquivalents`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Current Assets

**Definition:** Assets expected to be converted to cash, sold, or
consumed within the normal operating cycle / short term.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `AssetsCurrent`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Total Assets

**Definition:** Total economic resources reported on the company's
balance sheet.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `Assets`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Current Liabilities

**Definition:** Obligations generally expected to be settled within the
normal operating cycle / short term.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `LiabilitiesCurrent`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Total Liabilities

**Definition:** Total obligations reported by the company.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:** `Liabilities`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stockholders' Equity

**Definition:** Residual interest in the company's assets after
deducting liabilities.

**Typical SEC XBRL concepts:** - `StockholdersEquity` -
`StockholdersEquityIncludingPortionAttributableToNoncontrollingInterest`

### Accounting Equation

`Assets = Liabilities + Equity`

This equation is a useful internal consistency check for balance-sheet
data.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Financial Health Ratios

## Current Ratio

**Formula:**

`Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities`

**Interpretation:** Measures short-term liquidity. A higher or lower
value is not automatically good or bad; working-capital requirements
differ significantly by industry and business model.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Liabilities-to-Assets Ratio

**Formula:**

`Liabilities / Assets (%) = Total Liabilities / Total Assets × 100`

**Interpretation:** Shows the percentage of reported assets financed by
liabilities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Equity Ratio

**Formula:**

`Equity Ratio (%) = Stockholders' Equity / Total Assets × 100`

**Interpretation:** Shows the percentage of reported assets represented
by stockholders' equity.

For a simple balance sheet without complicating classification
differences:

`Liabilities-to-Assets % + Equity Ratio % ≈ 100%`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Cash Flow Metrics

## Operating Cash Flow (OCF)

**Definition:** Net cash provided by or used in the company's operating
activities.

**Typical SEC XBRL concept:**
`NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities`

**Why it matters:** Helps assess how much cash the core operations
actually generate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Capital Expenditures (CapEx)

**Definition:** Cash spent to acquire or improve property, plant,
equipment, and similar productive assets, based on the XBRL concept
selected by the tool.

**Typical SEC XBRL concepts:** -
`PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment` -
`PaymentsForPropertyPlantAndEquipment`

**Important:** The tool treats the SEC-reported CapEx value as a
positive cash expenditure for the FCF calculation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Free Cash Flow (FCF)

**Formula used by V1:**

`Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures`

**Interpretation:** Approximation of cash generated by operations after
spending on property, plant and equipment. It can be available for
purposes such as debt reduction, dividends, share repurchases,
acquisitions, or retaining cash.

**Important:** This is the tool's standardized V1 definition. Other
analysts/data providers may define FCF differently.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## FCF Margin

**Formula:**

`FCF Margin (%) = Free Cash Flow / Revenue × 100`

**Interpretation:** Measures how much free cash flow is produced for
each dollar of revenue.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Cash Conversion

**Formula used by V1:**

`Cash Conversion (%) = Operating Cash Flow / Net Income × 100`

**Interpretation:** Compares operating cash generation with reported net
income. It is useful for examining earnings quality and cash generation
over time.

A value above or below 100% is **not** automatically good or bad.
Working capital, depreciation, stock-based compensation, deferred
revenue, taxes, and other accounting/business factors can materially
affect the ratio.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Growth Formula Used by the Tool

For metrics such as Revenue, Gross Profit, Operating Income, Net Income,
and Diluted EPS:

`Growth (%) = ((Current Value - Previous Value) / |Previous Value|) × 100`

The absolute value of the previous period is used in the denominator by
the current V1 engine.

If there is no previous period, growth is displayed as `N/A`.

If the previous value is zero, growth is displayed as `N/A` to avoid
division by zero.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. SEC Data Normalization Rules

## Company Identification

The ticker is mapped to the SEC Central Index Key (CIK).

Example:

`AAPL → 0000320193`

The CIK is then used to request SEC Company Facts data.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Income Statement and Cash Flow

V1 primarily selects records where:

-   `form = 10-K`
-   `fp = FY`
-   requested unit matches the metric configuration

These statements describe activity **over a period**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Balance Sheet

Balance-sheet values are point-in-time facts. V1 uses the fiscal
period-end (`end`) date as the reporting date.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Duplicate SEC Facts

SEC Company Facts may contain repeated historical facts because later
filings include comparative prior-year values.

V1 groups eligible facts by period-end date and retains the most
recently filed eligible record for that period.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Metric Concept Mapping

Companies may use different valid US-GAAP XBRL concepts for economically
similar metrics.

The tool therefore defines an ordered list of candidate concepts and
selects the first available concept.

Example for Revenue:

1.  `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax`
2.  `Revenues`
3.  `SalesRevenueNet`

This mapping layer makes the tool more reusable across companies, but
results should still be validated when adding companies with different
reporting structures.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Interpretation Principles

The tool intentionally does **not** use simplistic rules such as:

-   Current Ratio \> 1 = automatically good
-   Current Ratio \< 1 = automatically bad
-   High liabilities = automatically bad
-   High equity = automatically good
-   Cash Conversion \> 100% = automatically good
-   Revenue growth = automatically a good investment

Instead, analysis should consider:

1.  Multi-year trends
2.  Business model
3.  Industry characteristics
4.  Growth consistency
5.  Profitability and margin trends
6.  Balance-sheet strength
7.  Cash generation
8.  Capital intensity
9.  Explanations in company filings
10. Valuation, which is outside the current Core V1 data engine

# 8. Core V1 Analysis Framework

-   Revenue Growth
-   Gross Profit Growth
-   Operating Income Growth
-   Net Income Growth
-   Diluted EPS Growth

## Profitability

-   Gross Margin
-   Operating Margin
-   Net Margin

## Financial Health

-   Cash & Cash Equivalents
-   Current Assets
-   Current Liabilities
-   Current Ratio
-   Total Assets
-   Total Liabilities
-   Stockholders' Equity
-   Liabilities-to-Assets Ratio
-   Equity Ratio

-   Operating Cash Flow
-   Capital Expenditures
-   Free Cash Flow
-   FCF Margin
-   Cash Conversion

# 9. Planned / Not Yet Included in Core V1

The following are intentionally not part of the current core
calculations yet:

-   Total Debt and Debt/Equity
-   ROE / ROA / ROIC
-   Share-count and buyback analysis
-   Quarterly / TTM analysis
-   Revenue segment analysis
-   Valuation metrics such as P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA and FCF Yield
-   Current market-price data
-   Automated buy/sell scoring

These can be added in later versions after the core SEC
financial-statement engine and Excel output are stable.
